# Biological Change After Harvest

## Purpose

This Engineering Extension connects the biological processes introduced in the book chapters about living cells, respiration, maintenance, oxidation, microorganisms, water, temperature, and time.

It does not replace those chapters. Its purpose is to examine what becomes visible when these processes are considered together after harvest.

The central engineering question is:

> How does biological material continue to change after harvest, and how can an engineered environment influence the rate and direction of that change?

## Connecting the Processes

The individual processes described in the book are not independent.

After harvest, several processes can continue simultaneously:

```text
Harvest
   |
   v
Living cells remain active
   |
   +----> Respiration
   |
   +----> Water movement
   |
   +----> Molecular reactions
   |
   +----> Oxidation
   |
   +----> Microbial activity
   |
   v
Changes in structure, quality and composition
```

The important observation is that preservation does not create a completely different biological system.

It changes the conditions under which the existing processes occur.

## From Biology to Engineering

Once these processes are considered together, an engineering question appears:

> Which environmental conditions can be changed in order to slow unwanted biological change while maintaining the desired characteristics of the product?

This question connects biological understanding with temperature control, humidity management, airflow, water management, monitoring, storage, and preservation.

The engineering system therefore does not control biology directly.

It controls or influences environmental conditions that affect biological processes.

## Why Measurement Becomes Important

If biological change depends on environmental conditions, those conditions become relevant observations.

This creates a chain:

```text
Biological process
      |
      v
Environmental influence
      |
      v
Observable condition
      |
      v
Measurement
      |
      v
Evidence
      |
      v
Engineering decision
```

This is one reason monitoring should begin with a biological question rather than with a list of available sensors.

## Engineering Questions That Follow

Connecting these processes leads to further questions:

- Which processes are most important immediately after harvest?
- Which processes are affected most strongly by temperature?
- How does water availability influence biological activity?
- When does microbial activity become a dominant concern?
- Which changes can be measured directly?
- Which changes require indirect evidence?
- How quickly do different processes respond to environmental changes?
- How can an intervention be validated rather than merely assumed to work?

These questions lead into the engineering domains of monitoring, measurement, climate control, water systems, energy, maintenance, and preservation.

## Boundary

This document does not define specific temperature ranges, sensors, sampling intervals, equipment, or control algorithms.

Those belong to the engineering implementation layer.

This Extension exists to preserve the reasoning that connects biological understanding to those later engineering decisions.
