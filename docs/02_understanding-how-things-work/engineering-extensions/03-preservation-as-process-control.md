# Preservation as Process Control

## Purpose

This Engineering Extension connects the book chapters about preservation, drying, cooling, freezing, freeze-drying, packaging, logistics, and time.

The purpose is to examine preservation as a process of controlling the conditions under which unwanted change occurs.

## Connecting the Processes

Preservation does not stop all change. It changes the conditions under which different processes occur.

```text
Biological material
        |
        v
Unwanted change
        |
        v
Identify controlling conditions
        |
        v
Temperature / water / oxygen / time
        |
        v
Measurement
        |
        v
Evidence
        |
        v
Engineering decision
```

The important engineering shift is therefore from asking which preservation method is available to asking which conditions need to be controlled to achieve the desired preservation objective.

## Engineering Questions That Follow

- Which changes are we trying to slow or prevent?
- Which environmental conditions influence those changes?
- Which conditions can actually be controlled?
- Which conditions can be measured reliably?
- How quickly does the product respond to a change in conditions?
- What trade-offs exist between preservation quality, energy use, cost, and throughput?
- How can preservation performance be demonstrated rather than assumed?

## Boundary

This document does not define specific preservation recipes, temperature settings, equipment specifications, or control algorithms.

Those belong to engineering implementation and experimental validation.

This Extension preserves the reasoning that connects the scientific understanding of preservation to later engineering decisions.
