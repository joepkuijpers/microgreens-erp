## Purpose

This Engineering Extension connects the book's explanations of water, molecular movement, temperature, drying, cooling, freezing, thawing, and freeze-drying.

The purpose is to understand why water is not simply a quantity that can be removed, added, frozen, or evaporated. Water participates in the structure and behaviour of biological material.

The central engineering question is:

> How does the physical state and availability of water influence biological material during processing and preservation?

## Water Is Part of the System

Water in biological material is not only a measurable quantity.

It can be:

- part of cellular and structural organisation;
- a medium in which chemical and biological processes occur;
- a carrier of heat;
- a component of mass transfer;
- a factor influencing biological activity;
- a factor influencing microbial activity.

These roles mean that changing the state or availability of water can change the behaviour of the material.

The engineering problem is therefore not simply:

> How much water can be removed?

A more useful question is:

> What happens to the material as water changes state, moves, or is removed?

## Molecular and Material Behaviour

Temperature influences molecular movement and therefore affects many physical and chemical processes.

Water can also exist in different physical states, and the transition between those states changes how energy and mass move through the material.
