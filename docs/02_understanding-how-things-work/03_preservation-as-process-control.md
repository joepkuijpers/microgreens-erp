# Preservation as Process Control

## Purpose

This Engineering Extension connects the book's explanations of biological change, temperature, water, time, drying, cooling, freezing, thawing, and freeze-drying.

The purpose is to understand preservation as an active process of influencing the conditions under which unwanted change occurs.

Preservation does not necessarily stop every biological, chemical, or physical process.

Instead, preservation changes the conditions that influence the speed, direction, or extent of those processes.

The central engineering question is:

> Which processes do we want to slow, which characteristics do we want to protect, and which environmental conditions can influence those outcomes?

## Preservation Changes Conditions

The processes that change biological material are influenced by their environment.

Temperature, water availability, oxygen exposure, time, pressure, and other conditions can influence different processes in different ways.

This means that preservation can be understood as changing the environment around the product.

Once preservation is viewed as process control, an important distinction becomes visible.

The objective is not simply to make the product colder, drier, or otherwise different.

The objective is to create conditions that favour the desired preservation outcome.

For example, reducing temperature may slow some processes, while freezing introduces a different physical state. Removing water can reduce the availability of water for certain processes, while freeze-drying combines freezing, reduced pressure, and controlled water removal.

The engineering problem therefore becomes a problem of relationships rather than isolated settings.
