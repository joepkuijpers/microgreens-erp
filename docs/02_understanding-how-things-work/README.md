# Preservation Energy Architecture

Preservation Energy Architecture explains food preservation by beginning with biology.

The collection follows biological organisation from living plants through harvest, deterioration, preservation, packaging, logistics, preparation and human use.

Its central principle is:

> Technology should adapt to biology, not biology to technology.

Engineering becomes more effective when the system being supported is understood first.

## Purpose

These chapters connect biology, chemistry, physics, engineering and human responsibility.

They explain:

- what preservation is trying to protect;
- why harvested food continues to change;
- how water, temperature, oxygen, microorganisms and time influence quality;
- how preservation methods alter the conditions in which change occurs;
- why packaging and logistics remain part of preservation;
- how food ultimately supports human biology.

The collection explains why before how.

It provides a conceptual foundation for research and engineering decisions rather than instructions for operating equipment.

## Scope

This collection provides explanatory knowledge.

It does not replace:

- food-safety procedures;
- validated operating instructions;
- equipment manuals;
- engineering calculations;
- legal or regulatory requirements;
- crop-specific research;
- laboratory validation.

Operational decisions should remain connected to measured evidence, documented risk and appropriate professional guidance.

## Relationship to the Project

The Project Foundation defines why the Microgreens ERP project exists and which principles guide it.

Preservation Energy Architecture develops the biological and scientific understanding required to apply those principles responsibly.

Engineering documentation translates that understanding into practical systems, measurements and equipment.

Together, these documentation layers connect purpose, understanding and implementation.
