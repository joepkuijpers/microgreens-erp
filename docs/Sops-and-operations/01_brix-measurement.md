# Brix measurement

## Status

Draft — validate with the selected refractometer and the first controlled microgreen trials before routine use.

## Purpose

This procedure describes how to measure the refractive index of expressed microgreen sap and record the result in degrees Brix (°Bx).

The purpose is to create repeatable observations that can be compared within the same crop, growth stage and sampling method.

A Brix reading is not a complete measure of plant health, nutritional value or sugar content. It reflects how strongly dissolved substances in the sample affect refraction, expressed on a sucrose-equivalent scale.

## Scope

This procedure applies to destructive sap measurements from microgreens and other young plant tissue.

It may support:

- comparisons between cultivation batches;
- comparisons between rack positions;
- observation of changes during growth;
- evaluation of controlled cultivation trials;
- comparison with harvest quality and environmental conditions.

Results from different crops, plant parts, growth stages or sampling methods should not be compared without validation.

## Principle

A refractometer measures how light changes direction when it passes through a liquid sample.

The result is displayed in degrees Brix. In pure sucrose solutions, 1 °Bx corresponds to approximately 1 gram of sucrose per 100 grams of solution. Plant sap contains other dissolved substances, including organic acids, minerals and amino acids. The reading must therefore be treated as a refractive soluble-solids indicator rather than a direct chemical sugar analysis.

## Required equipment

- A clean refractometer with an appropriate Brix range and resolution.
- Distilled or demineralised water for calibration.
- A clean plant press, garlic press or equivalent sap-extraction tool.
- Clean sample containers or surfaces.
- Lint-free tissue suitable for the prism.
- Clean water for equipment cleaning.
- The ERP measurement form or a temporary measurement record.
- Gloves where required by hygiene procedures.

The instrument manufacturer's instructions take precedence for calibration, temperature range, cleaning and sample volume.

## Measurement statement

Before sampling, record the question the measurement is intended to support.

Define:

- crop and cultivar;
- grow batch;
- growth stage or days since sowing;
- sampling date and time;
- rack, shelf and sampling position;
- plant part;
- sampling pattern;
- number of plants or sample mass;
- time since irrigation or misting;
- comparison or decision being investigated.

Without this context, the result has limited long-term value.

## Sampling rules

Plant sap can vary with plant part, water status, growth stage, light exposure and time of day. Sampling must therefore remain consistent.

For microgreens:

1. Select plants from predefined positions.
2. Use the same plant part and cutting height for every comparable measurement.
3. Combine several plants into one pooled sample when one plant provides insufficient sap.
4. Prepare at least three independent pooled samples for a comparative measurement.
5. Keep the number of plants or sample mass consistent between samples.
6. Do not include growing medium, seed hulls, standing water or visible contamination.
7. Do not sample visibly wet plants. If surface moisture is present, record the condition and postpone the comparison where possible.
8. Record unusual conditions rather than silently correcting them.

A pooled sample is one replicate. Plants used in one replicate must not be reused in another.

## Calibration

Before the measurement session:

1. Clean the prism and allow it to dry.
2. Apply distilled or demineralised water according to the instrument instructions.
3. Confirm or adjust the instrument to 0.0 °Bx.
4. Remove the water and dry the prism without scratching it.
5. Record whether calibration passed.
6. Repeat calibration when the instrument, room or sample temperature changes substantially, or when readings appear unstable.

For automatic temperature compensation, remain within the range specified by the manufacturer. Temperature compensation does not correct inconsistent sampling or surface water.

## Procedure

For each replicate:

1. Confirm the sample identity and position.
2. Place the plant material in the clean press.
3. Express enough sap for the refractometer without adding water.
4. Apply the required sample volume to the prism.
5. Ensure the sample covers the measurement area without bubbles or visible particles.
6. Allow the reading to stabilise according to the instrument instructions.
7. Record the displayed value in °Bx.
8. Record sample or instrument temperature when available.
9. Repeat the instrument reading when the boundary is unclear or the value is unstable.
10. Clean and dry the prism and press before the next replicate.

Never dilute a sample unless a separate validated dilution procedure and calculation are recorded.

## Replicates and result

Record every raw replicate separately.

For a routine comparative measurement, calculate:

- number of replicates;
- mean Brix value;
- minimum value;
- maximum value;
- spread or standard deviation when useful.

Do not replace raw readings with only an average. Variation between replicates may reveal uneven growth, inconsistent sampling or instrument problems.

## ERP record

Each measurement record should include:

- measurement date and time;
- grow batch;
- crop profile;
- growth stage or crop age;
- rack and sampling position;
- plant part;
- sampling method;
- sample size or mass;
- replicate number;
- Brix value;
- instrument identifier;
- instrument resolution;
- temperature-compensation status;
- calibration status;
- time since irrigation or misting;
- observer;
- notes;
- experimental or routine status.

Environmental readings may be linked by time and batch, but they must not be copied into the Brix result as though they were measured by the refractometer.

## Interpretation

Use Brix primarily for comparisons made with the same method.

A higher or lower reading may reflect differences in:

- dissolved carbohydrates;
- organic acids;
- minerals and other soluble compounds;
- plant water status;
- crop age;
- sampling position;
- light and time of day;
- recent irrigation;
- extraction method;
- temperature or instrument behaviour.

A single reading must not be labelled healthy or unhealthy. Pest resistance, flavour, nutrient density and overall crop quality require additional evidence.

Project-specific reference ranges may only be created after sufficient repeated measurements have been compared with plant observations, cultivation conditions and harvest outcomes.

## Invalid measurements

Mark a measurement invalid rather than deleting it when:

- calibration failed;
- the sample was diluted unintentionally;
- visible water or growing medium contaminated the sample;
- the plant part or sampling position is unknown;
- the reading was outside the instrument range;
- the instrument did not stabilise;
- the measurement cannot be linked to the intended batch;
- the procedure differed in a way that prevents comparison.

Record the reason for invalidation.

## Cleaning and storage

After use:

1. Remove all plant material from the press.
2. Clean the prism using the method approved by the manufacturer.
3. Dry the prism with a non-abrasive material.
4. Clean and dry the extraction tools.
5. Store the refractometer in its protective case.
6. Record damage, drift or calibration problems.

## Initial validation

Before this procedure becomes routine:

1. Select the refractometer.
2. Confirm its range, resolution and temperature-compensation behaviour.
3. Determine the practical microgreen sample size.
4. Compare repeated samples from the same batch.
5. Test variation across rack positions and times.
6. Compare results with visible plant condition and harvest observations.
7. Document any method changes.

The validated method should favour repeatability over impressive-looking values.
